## Q: Describe a challenging project you worked on.

One challenging project was improving the Salesforce release process using Copado and Gearset. We were coordinating changes across multiple Salesforce orgs, with many User Stories and dependencies moving through Developer orgs, QA, UAT and Production. The process required careful conflict management, validation, approvals and back promotion.

I helped improve the release workflow, strengthen quality gates and make the promotion process more predictable. We handled approximately 60 to 80 User Stories in a major release and reduced the release window from about two days to 6 to 7 hours. The main lesson was that release speed comes from improving the full process, including preparation, dependency management, validation and communication, rather than simply trying to deploy faster.

## Q: Tell me about a difficult production issue you resolved.

During a release, a deployment can fail because a component depends on metadata that is missing or not yet available in the target org. In one such situation, I focused on the deployment error and identified the missing dependency rather than treating it as a general deployment failure. I coordinated with the developer to confirm the intended change, made sure the required metadata was included in the correct order, and ran validation again before proceeding with deployment.

After deployment, I verified the affected functionality and communicated the result to the team. My approach is to identify the root cause, understand the impact, correct the source of the issue and validate the fix before moving forward. I avoid making an untracked change directly in Production.

## Q: Tell me about a time you handled a disagreement with a team member.

We had a situation where two developers changed the same Apex class in separate User Stories. There were different opinions about which version should be retained during conflict resolution. Instead of choosing one version based on who raised the issue first, I brought the developers together and reviewed the changes and the intended functionality in each story.

We agreed on the combined outcome, resolved the conflict in the source-controlled change and validated it before promotion. It reinforced that conflict resolution should be based on business and technical requirements, not simply on accepting one side of a merge.

## Q: How do you handle pressure during production releases?

I stay structured and focus on the release checklist. Before deployment, I confirm the scope, approvals, dependencies, validation results, communication plan and rollback or forward-fix approach. During deployment, I monitor the logs and coordinate with the relevant developers and QA members. If an issue occurs, I assess its impact and communicate clearly rather than making rushed changes.

After deployment, I make sure smoke testing and post-deployment verification are completed. Staying calm, following the process and keeping stakeholders informed helps the team make good decisions under pressure.

## Q: How do you manage multiple priorities?

I prioritize based on business impact, urgency, dependencies and risk. Production issues and release blockers come first, followed by committed release activities and planned improvements. I break larger work into manageable tasks, agree on priorities with stakeholders and communicate early if there is a conflict or a delivery risk.

I also look for opportunities to automate repetitive work so the team can spend more time on higher-value activities.

## Q: Have you worked with offshore and onsite teams?

Yes. I have worked with distributed teams across development, QA and release management. My approach is to make responsibilities, timelines and handoffs clear. I use documented deployment steps, issue details and release updates so that work can continue across time zones without depending on informal conversations.

For important releases, I make sure blockers, approvals and ownership are visible to everyone involved. Clear documentation and timely communication are especially important when teams are not working at the same time.

## Q: How do you handle tight release deadlines?

I first confirm what is essential for the release and what can safely be deferred. Then I check dependencies, testing status, approvals and deployment risks. I do not recommend skipping critical quality gates just to meet a date. If a change is not ready, I discuss the impact with the release owner and stakeholders and agree whether to fix it, defer it or use an approved expedited process.

The goal is to meet the business deadline without creating a bigger production issue.
